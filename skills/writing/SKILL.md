# Write Clear Technical Prose

## Establish the Reader and Claim

- Name the intended reader and define terms or abbreviations once.
- Use verbs that match the evidence. Cite verifiable sources for factual claims; never invent
  citations.
- Calibrate stated confidence to the evidence: hedge once and precisely where uncertainty is real
  rather than reflexively everywhere.

## Make Sentences Direct

- Prefer active voice when the actor matters, concrete nouns, plain English, and positive phrasing.
- Cut filler, clichés, needless qualifiers, and casual metaphors.
- Keep subjects near verbs and modifiers near what they modify. Split long sentences and vary
  sentence length.

## Structure for Reading

- Keep related ideas together and use parallel grammar for parallel ideas.
- Put the important or new information at a sentence’s end.
- Use headings and bullets only when they reveal real structure; use prose for connected reasoning.
- Keep terminology consistent, use title case for headings, and avoid contractions in formal prose.
- Avoid repeated sentence openings, stock transitions, casual dashes, and automatic summary closers.

Apply these rules as clarity aids, not rigid rules. Preserve a deliberate voice when breaking one
improves the prose.
